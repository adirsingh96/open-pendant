What arrives from JLCPCB, and what you solder

The carrier is a bare 23 × 38 mm board. JLCPCB fabricates the copper. You mount the XIAO you already have, a microSD socket, one capacitor, one button, and the cell wires. Read this before you upload anything.

What JLCPCB puts in the package

In the JLCPCB box

Empty boards, usually five

Five is the usual minimum. Each one is a green (or whatever mask colour you pick) rectangle, 23 mm wide, 38 mm long, 1.6 mm thick. The pads are already tinned with HASL. The white silk shows USB, C1, SW1, and on the back BAT+ and BAT−.

No XIAO, no socket, no capacitor, no button, no battery, no case. Nothing is soldered. A stencil comes only if you add one to the order.

Not from JLCPCB

The electronics, from you

The board is the wiring. The parts are separate. The XIAO is the module you already run firmware on. The socket, capacitor, and button are small parts you buy (LCSC is the easy source, and they can ride along in the same checkout without being soldered). The cell is a 1S LiPo, 3.7 V, about 6 × 20 × 30 mm (a 7 × 20 × 25 mm cell fits the same pads). The case to print is the Orbit O in hardware/openpendant_carrier/cad/character/. Read PRINT.txt there.

Parts you have to have on the bench

PartWhat to buyWhere it goesHow hard
XIAO nRF52840 Sense The module you already have. It must be the flat SMD version, with no pin headers. Front, USB jack hanging off the short edge marked USB. Edge pins are a normal iron fillet. One pad under the module needs paste or hot air.
microSD socket Molex 104031-0811 (LCSC C585350). A connector, not a breakout board. Front, mouth pointing off the end opposite the USB. Contacts are 1.1 mm apart. Flux, a fine tip, and solder wick.
22 µF capacitor 0805 ceramic, 6.3 V or 10 V, X5R or X7R. No stripe, no polarity. Front, in the gap between the XIAO and the socket. Silk says C1. The easy one.
Button A TS-1088 tactile switch, about 4 × 3 mm, pads 4.45 mm apart. Example: LCSC C720477. Front, on the right, silk SW1. The Ø6 button from the strip does not fit. Two pads. Easy.
Cell Your WLY602030. Red is positive, black is negative. Open wires, no connector. Two pads on the back, in the open bay at the USB end. The cell body starts above that bay, wire end facing the pads. An iron. Cut the leads short, strip 2.5 mm, and follow the silk.

Tools: a fine-tip iron, flux gel, 0.5 mm solder, tweezers, solder wick, and a magnifier. For the one hidden battery pad, solder paste and a hot-air gun, or a small hot plate. An iron alone cannot reach that pad once the XIAO is sitting on it.

Why assembly stays off

You already own a working XIAO. The factory would need a second, bare one, plus a setup fee, and you would still want to check the board yourself first. Five bare boards are the cheap experiment. The hundred-dollar quote was the other board.

A stencil is optional. It only helps if you want to paste the whole XIAO at once. A syringe of paste on the single hidden pad is enough.

How the XIAO actually connects

There is no connector and no header. The XIAO sits flat on the front pads and you solder its castellations, the little half-holes along both long edges. Those fillets are the electrical connection for the SD card, the button, 3.3 V, and ground.

If the XIAO on your strip has pin headers, they have to come off before it will sit on this board. The carrier has no holes for those pins. Desolder them, or keep that XIAO on the strip and put a header-free Sense module on the carrier.

Assembly, in order

  1. Look at a bare board before any solder. One short edge is marked USB. The SD pads are at the other short edge. Flip it. Two pads near the USB end are marked BAT+ and BAT−. If the silk is missing or the outline is not 23 × 38 mm, stop.
  2. Tin battery positive before the XIAO goes down. On the front, the BAT+ pad is the small rectangle that will sit under the left half of the module, not the big back-side wire pad. Put a small dome of solder or a dot of paste on that pad, and tin the matching + pad on the bottom of the XIAO. Too much solder tilts the module.
  3. Place the XIAO and tack two corners. Component face up, antenna and mic away from the USB. The USB-C jack hangs off the USB edge of the board. The castellations should sit centered on the red pads, the same amount sticking out on the left and the right. Tack one corner, check, then tack the opposite corner.
  4. Melt the hidden BAT+ joint. Hot air on that area, or a hot plate under the board, while you press the module flat with tweezers. You will not see the joint. When it has cooled, the module should not rock. Later, a meter from the back BAT+ pad to the XIAO’s BAT+ pad should beep. If it does not, the cell will not charge and will not power the board.
  5. Solder the fourteen edge pads with the iron. Flux first, then a small fillet on each castellation. Drag solder is fine if you wick any bridge. D4 and D5 are the motion sensor and must stay unconnected to anything except their own pad. 5V stays a pad with no wire. Solder it only to hold the module.
  6. Capacitor, then button. C1 is the 0805 between the XIAO and the socket. Either orientation. SW1 is the button on the right. Two pads, no polarity, button facing up. The round 6 mm button from the perfboard strip does not fit these pads.
  7. Socket last, so the iron has room. Metal shell on the front. The mouth and the card guide point off the top edge, away from the USB. Tack the big shield tabs first so it cannot shift, then the contact row. 1.1 mm pitch. If two contacts bridge, flux and wick. Do not scrape the contacts with the iron.
  8. Cell wires on the back, in the open bay. Back of the board facing you, USB end toward you. The left pad is BAT+ and the right pad is BAT−, and the silk says so. Turn the cell so the wires come out of the end nearest those pads. Cut each lead to about 10 mm, strip 2.5 mm, and lay the bare end flat on the pad. The insulation sits in the gap between the pad and the cell. It does not tuck under the cell: there is no room there. Red on the left, black on the right. Those pads are already connected to the XIAO. Do not also touch the cell to the 5V pad, and do not add a separate charger. The charger is the one already on the XIAO.

Back, the way the wires go on

Check it before you print the case

  1. No cell yet. Plug USB. The XIAO should show up on the computer the same way it does today. The charge LED is the small light by the USB jack.
  2. Meter: back BAT+ to the XIAO BAT+ pad beeps. Back BAT− beeps to a GND castellation. BAT+ does not beep to GND.
  3. Push a microSD in from the top until it seats. The firmware already talks SPI on D6, D7, D8, and D9. The card should mount. Pull to remove. This socket does not click-to-eject.
  4. Then the cell. Red and black as marked. USB should still charge it. Unplug USB and the board should stay on from the cell.
  5. The button is D1, active low. A press should do what the button does on the strip.

The printed shells are a separate job, after this works. Print character_front.3mf, character_back.stl, and character_button_hat.stl from hardware/openpendant_carrier/cad/character/. The print settings, snap, USB opening, and chain bail are in PRINT.txt in that folder. JLCPCB does not make the case.

The decision, plainly

Easy with an iron

The capacitor, the button, the two cell wires in the back bay, and the XIAO’s edge fillets, if you have flux and a fine tip.

Needs care

The SD socket’s 1.1 mm contacts. Bridges are normal the first time. Wick fixes them. Nothing on this board is a chip you can cook by holding the iron in one place.

The one new skill

Battery positive is a pad you tin, then melt, under the XIAO. If you do not want hot air or a hot plate, do not order yet. Everything else can wait with it.
